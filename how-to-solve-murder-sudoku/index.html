How to solve a murder sudoku, step by step

In The Scene you get the floor plan of a house, a few suspects and a handful of clues. You place everyone where they stood, and whoever ends up alone with the body is guilty. Here are the rules and a real case, solved all the way through.

The rules

  • Every suspect stands on one square of the plan.
  • One per row and one per column. With four suspects on a four-by-four plan, every row and every column holds exactly one person.
  • One per room: two people are never in the same room.
  • Furniture takes up its square: nobody stands on it.
  • The killer is whoever was alone with the victim, meaning the only person in the room with the body.

What the clues mean

  • "Was in the kitchen" or "was not in the chapel" is about the whole room.
  • "Was in the same row as the corkscrew" is that row, not counting the furniture's own square.
  • "Was near the olive tree" means the squares next to it, but only inside its room: walls cut it off.
  • "Was somewhere in column D" is exactly that.

In the app, tap a clue and the plan lights up the squares it talks about.

A real case: harvest night

The daily case from 21 September 2026, rated easy. Harvest night at the Solana estate ends with the presses still running and the estate owner Amelia dead among the barrels.

The suspects:

  • Iris, the sommelier
  • Crisp, the cellar master
  • Rupert, the nephew
  • Dove, the cook

The clues:

  1. Rupert was nowhere near the giant corkscrew.
  2. Crisp was in the same row as the giant corkscrew.
  3. Crisp was not in the same column as the old press wheel.
  4. Iris was not in the Chapel.
  5. Dove was somewhere in column D.

Step by step

  1. Start with the tightest clue. Crisp was in the same row as the giant corkscrew, which is on A2. So Crisp is in row 2: B2, C2 or D2.
  2. Crisp was not in the same column as the old press wheel, on B1. B2 is out, which leaves C2 and D2.
  3. Dove was somewhere in column D. Only one person fits in a column, so column D belongs to Dove and Crisp cannot be on D2. Crisp is on C2.
  4. C2 is the kitchen, and the body is in the kitchen. With one person per room, you already know who was alone with the victim. Finish the plan anyway: an accusation only stands if everything fits.
  5. Iris and Rupert are left, with columns A and B. In column B, B1 is the press wheel and B4 is kitchen, which already has Crisp: only B3 remains. In column A, A2 is the corkscrew, and A3 and A4 are chapel, like B3. Only one person fits in the chapel, so whoever takes column A has to be on A1.
  6. Iris was not in the chapel, so she cannot be on B3. Iris is on A1 and Rupert on B3.
  7. Rows 1, 2 and 3 are taken, so Dove is on D4.

Crisp, the cellar master, was alone with the estate owner in the kitchen. Guilty.

What about Rupert's clue? "Rupert was nowhere near the giant corkscrew" was not needed on this route. That happens a lot: a clue that is spare in one line of reasoning is the key to another. When you are stuck, go back to the ones you have not used yet.

Detective mode

It is on by default: the body stays off the plan until you have placed everyone, and its square counts like any other. Putting someone on it is simply a wrong placement. The reasoning does not change: at the end the body appears and tells you who was with it.

Tips

  • Start with clues that say where someone was, not where they were not.
  • Count the gaps: if a row, a column or a room has only one square left for someone, it is theirs.
  • Use the pencil: pick a suspect and long-press a square to rule it out.
  • You get three free checks a day to see whether you are on track.
